How to read this table

A box spread is a loan built from S&P 500 index options: cash today, one fixed repayment at expiration. Because arbitrageurs compete to earn the risk-free rate on the other side of the trade, the implied borrowing rate tracks the Treasury yield of the matching maturity. Pick your term above, and the number next to it is approximately the market's wholesale cost of money for that period. That, give or take a small spread, is the rate a well-executed box prices at.

Compare that to what banks charge against the same collateral. Securities-backed lines of credit typically run several points above Treasuries, variable. Margin loans run higher still. The box spread removes the middleman, which is the entire point.

Why the rate is set by the market

There is no loan officer in a box spread. The four option legs create a fixed payoff at expiration, so whoever buys the other side is simply lending at the implied rate. Index arbitrage desks do this at scale, competing with each other, and that competition pins the rate to Treasuries. A bank's rate committee never enters the picture. That is why the table above, a public government data feed, is a working benchmark for what this financing costs.

What this means in dollars

On $1,000,000 borrowed for a year, each percentage point of rate is $10,000. A borrower refinancing a 7.5% SBLOC into a box priced near the 1-year benchmark above keeps the difference, every year, on the same collateral. Frequently asked questions

What determines box spread rates?

The options market. The implied rate is set by arbitrageurs competing to earn the risk-free rate, so it tracks U.S. Treasury yields of the same maturity, typically within a few tenths of a percent. No bank sets or marks up the rate.

Are box spread rates fixed or variable?

Fixed. The rate locks the moment the trade executes and does not change for the life of the position, whether that is 30 days or several years. SBLOC and margin rates are variable and move with the bank's reference rate.

How close are box spread rates to Treasury yields?

Historically, implied box rates on broad index options have landed within a few tenths of a percent of comparable-maturity Treasuries. The exact spread varies with term, market conditions, and execution quality.

Is the cost tax-deductible?

The financing cost is generally treated as a Section 1256 capital loss rather than interest expense, which may offset capital gains regardless of how the money is used. Tax treatment depends on your circumstances; consult your tax adviser.
