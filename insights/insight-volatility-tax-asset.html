Volatility Is the Price of Stocks. Get Paid Back.

In a taxable account, the swings are not just a cost to endure. Owned as individual stocks, they become raw material you convert into cash value.

Volatility is the price you pay for owning stocks. In a taxable account, you can get paid back.

Everyone treats volatility as something to endure. Grit your teeth, ride it out, do not look. There is truth to that. But viewed through a different lens, volatility is also something you can convert into cash value.

One ETF Hides It. Individual Stocks Surface It.

Own the S&P as one ETF and a choppy year that ends up 10% is just a quiet 10%. Nothing to do. Own the same index as individual stocks and that year is full of opportunities.

Under a 10% index, names are down 15% in March, down 20% in April, back to even by July. Every one of those dips is a harvest.

The Move

Sell the name that is down. Bank the loss. Replace it with similar exposure so the portfolio never actually leaves the market. What changes is that the loss is now a permanent tax asset, sitting on your books, waiting to be used.

The more volatile the year, the bigger the harvest. The scary markets may pay the most.

That is the part most people have backwards. Volatility stops being the tax you pay to own stocks. It becomes the thing working for you while you wait.

It Has to Be Run Well

None of this is automatic. It takes security-level tax management, careful attention to the wash sale rule, and discipline held across every kind of market. But managed properly, every harvested loss is a tax asset, banked and carried forward, waiting for the event that triggers a big gain: a business sale, a concentrated position unwind, an RSU vest, an appreciated property.

If you are sitting through the swings and collecting nothing for it, there is a better use for that volatility.
